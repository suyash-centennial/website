## The assignment

The study determines what Asia as a whole is capable of achieving by the year 2050, and the related policies and reforms required to reach its development potential.

It focuses on the importance of Asian policy makers looking beyond their national borders. Managing some of the biggest risks facing the region, particularly cross-country disparities that could lead to conflict, will require region-wide discussion and action. As the center of gravity of the world economy moves to Asia, culminating in its share of global GDP rising to half or more, a singular focus on domestic economic and social development will no longer be possible or desirable.

## Services provided

The study examines how countries can build, maintain and connect infrastructure to support sustainable regional economic growth, explains how inadequate coverage and quality of infrastructure services create bottlenecks that constrain growth in many parts of the region, and explores the role of the private sector in generating the resources needed to address the region's infrastructure deficit.

It discusses infrastructure in the contexts of urbanization, inclusive growth and energy security, considers how improvements in these areas can lead to greater inclusion and a reduction in poverty and inequality, and proposes policies and actions to achieve them.
