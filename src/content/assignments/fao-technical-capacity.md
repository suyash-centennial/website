## The assignment

In 2012 FAO underwent a strategic transformation that created a matrix management structure organizing human resources by strategic objective and devolved more of the organization's work from headquarters in Rome to country and regional offices. Because this took place with a flat budget, FAO's governing body was concerned that technical capacity had declined, hampering the organization's normative work. The assessment quantitatively examined the full range of human resources, including budgeted staff, staff funded through trust funds, and non-staff consultants.

It found that while overall staff numbers had declined, technical staff contributing to normative work had grown at headquarters and in regional offices, owing to the redeployment of administrative posts and streamlining, and that FAO had hired more technical consultants. Indicators of normative delivery improved across the board, findings corroborated by a review of contemporary assessments of FAO, including by MOPAN.

## Services provided

Using data from FAO's human resources department, the team analyzed staffing over 2012–2016 by grade, department and nature of work, and then quantitatively assessed FAO's products and services, including publications, missions, South-South cooperation agreements and the reach of flagship reports. The report set out the findings, reviewed FAO's overall human resources structure, and recommended improvements to data collection. It was informed by a mission to FAO headquarters.
